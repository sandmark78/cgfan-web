## Prompt

A Cannes-level four-panel confectionery technology campaign poster for a fictional premium brand called AURELITH, built as one refined 2x2 grid with the exact visual discipline of an industrial luxury food advertisement. Preserve the core structural logic of a modular poster series: each quadrant is an independent product scene with a restrained headline zone near the top, a hero product composition in the middle, and a tiny minimal brand mark placed quietly near the lower edge. Reduce typography to the absolute minimum. No body copy paragraphs, no marketing blocks, no loud captions. Only one short English headline per panel, two words maximum, plus one tiny minimal logo mark and one micro line of technical labeling. The overall feeling is colder, quieter, more curated, with larger negative space and more measured breathing room.
Panel 1: a warm taupe-to-cream field with three wafer cup forms lifted on slender vertical stems, each holding a different flowing confection material: satin caramel, translucent ruby syrup, and dense dark chocolate. The liquid ribbons hang with physically exact gravity, elegant tension, clean tapering ends, and mirror-like edible highlights. Panel 2: a pale powder-blue sculpted drape with long soft folds, holding several layered nut bars and chocolate-coated bar fragments placed sparsely and precisely across the surface, like museum objects. Panel 3: a cool light-grey abstract landscape of smooth rounded mounds with sandwich biscuits and cream-filled chocolate cookies staged with generous spacing, one upright hero piece and a few opened pieces revealing crisp filling texture and fine crumb detail. Panel 4: a deep restrained cobalt field with one iconic vertical chocolate gesture, a spoon-like molten stream forming a central axis, surrounded by a few suspended molded chocolates and small fragments, controlled and sparse rather than explosive.
Use elevated frontal product photography with disciplined central weighting, rule of thirds, clean symmetry tension, and expansive negative space. The objects must remain visually dominant at all times. The composition should feel like Orbit, Transit, and Port merged into one master solution: emotionally magnetic, materially exact, and curatorially reduced. The image language is hyperreal commercial still life, not illustration, with a calm futuristic sensibility. Lighting is layered and soft: upper-front key light, low-intensity fill for shadow readability, delicate specular control, smooth tonal transitions, subtle atmospheric separation, strong volume without heavy blacks. Keep the campaign cooler and quieter than a typical confection ad, with restrained contrast and clean air around every object.
Materials must be deeply believable and tactile: tempered chocolate with smooth reflective bloom and dense body, wafer shells with fine porous texture and crisp edges, nougat bars with visible nut fragmentation and layered compression, cream fillings with soft whipped density, sculpted set surfaces in matte ceramic-plaster finish, subtle micro imperfections that increase realism. Color hierarchy: 60% cocoa, cream, and caramel browns, 30% cool architectural backdrop tones, 10% sharp glossy highlights. The surfaces are immaculate, premium, and uncluttered.
Typography must behave like graphic architecture, not decoration. Use elegant thin-to-medium weight sans-serif English type, carefully kerned, integrated into the empty zones, aligned with the grid, never crossing the core product recognition area. Headline direction: one minimal phrase per panel such as “Flow Study”, “Quiet Structure”, “Measured Bite”, “Liquid Form”. The logo is abstract, geometric, tiny, and discreet. The final image must feel like a luxury design annual cover story merged with a food technology campaign: minimal, exact, silent, premium, sculptural, sharply art-directed, physically plausible, cleanly printed, with no clutter, no excessive text, no random decorative particles, no busy branding, no dead shadows, no muddy gradients, no deformed food geometry, no broken letters, no chaotic composition.

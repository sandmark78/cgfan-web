## Prompt

——

Create a premium vertical 9:16 cinematic beauty editorial photograph.

SUBJECT:
A beautiful young East Asian woman in an extremely intimate close-up portrait.
Only part of her face is visible in frame.

She has refined natural East Asian facial features, dark brown-black hair, with a few loose strands naturally crossing the forehead and cheek.

Her expression is quiet, calm, slightly distant and introspective.
She is not posing for the camera.
Her gaze is slightly downward or away from the lens.

Natural soft pink lips, minimal makeup, almost no visible cosmetic styling.
The beauty should come primarily from skin, light, texture and atmosphere.

COMPOSITION — CRITICAL:
Extremely intimate close-up framing.

The face occupies most of the upper and middle frame.

One eye, part of the nose, cheek and lips become the main visual anchors.

Do not show the full face perfectly.

Allow the composition to crop:
- part of the forehead
- part of the cheek
- part of the lips
- part of the jaw
- part of the hair

Do not center the face perfectly.

The composition should feel accidental, elegant and editorial rather than conventionally beautiful.

Leave intentional negative space somewhere in the image for a very small line of typography.

The image should feel like a real luxury skincare editorial, independent fashion magazine beauty story or experimental beauty campaign photographed by a real photographer.

Not a normal portrait.
Not a commercial beauty headshot.

SKIN — CRITICAL:
The skin must look extremely realistic and tactile.

Visible:
- natural pores
- micro skin texture
- fine facial texture
- tiny irregularities
- subtle peach fuzz
- natural tonal variation
- slight imperfections

Most importantly:

The skin must have a clearly visible hydrated water-oil sheen.

It should look as if a very thin transparent layer of:
skincare essence,
facial oil,
serum,
moisture
or freshly applied hydrating treatment
is naturally sitting on the surface of the skin.

This creates a continuous translucent moisture film across the face.

The skin should appear:
hydrated,
slightly wet,
luminous,
supple,
freshly moisturized,
softly reflective.

Create glossy specular highlights across:
- cheekbones
- eyelids
- nose bridge
- side of the nose
- forehead
- upper lip
- lower lip
- chin

Tiny irregular micro-highlights should follow the real topology of the skin.

Some areas can appear almost mirror-like,
while other areas remain softly diffused.

The shine must NOT erase the skin texture.

Pores should still remain visible underneath the water-oil layer.

Do NOT create plastic skin.
Do NOT over-retouch.
Do NOT smooth away pores.
Do NOT make the skin uniformly glossy.

The final skin effect should feel like:

REAL SKIN
+
MOISTURE
+
FACIAL OIL
+
SERUM
+
WARM LIGHT

The skin is luminous because real light is interacting with moisture on the skin surface.

It should never look like digitally painted gloss.

LIGHT:
Warm late-afternoon or early-evening sunlight.

Soft golden beige, champagne, pale peach and muted blush tones.

The light enters strongly from the side and slightly behind the subject.

A narrow beam of sunlight may graze across:
- cheek
- eye
- eyelid
- nose
- lips
- forehead

Create strong contrast between glowing highlights and soft natural shadow.

The highlights should remain luminous and slightly overexposed in places.

The shadows remain deep, warm and transparent.

Do not flatten the lighting.

BACKLIGHT + LENS FLARE — CRITICAL:
Strong warm backlight must enter directly into the camera lens.

The light source may sit just outside the frame or partially inside the edge of the frame.

Allow the backlight to naturally create real optical artifacts:

- veiling flare
- lens flare
- analog halation
- highlight bloom
- lens ghosting
- light leaks
- washed luminous areas
- translucent haze
- soft overexposure
- subtle rainbow prism fragments
- pale champagne glow
- soft pink flare
- peach-orange light haze

IMPORTANT:

The lens flare does NOT need to avoid the face.

Allow the flare to move directly across the face.

It can pass across:
- one eye
- both eyes
- cheek
- forehead
- nose
- lips
- jaw

Parts of the face may temporarily lose contrast beneath the flare.

The flare may partially obscure facial details.

Do NOT preserve perfect facial clarity everywhere.

Some areas of the face can dissolve softly into light.

The flare should sometimes appear directly ON TOP OF the face, creating a translucent veil of light between the viewer and the woman.

This is intentional.

Bright highlights should softly bleed into surrounding areas instead of ending sharply.

The image should have beautiful analog highlight bloom and subtle halation.

The light should feel physical.

It should feel as if real sunlight is entering the lens and overwhelming the optics slightly.

Never make the lens flare look like a digital overlay or artificial Photoshop effect.

OPTICAL FOREGROUND — CRITICAL:
Place transparent or translucent optical material extremely close to the camera lens.

Possible material:
- thin transparent film
- clear plastic sheet
- translucent acrylic
- glass
- transparent fabric
- thin prism-like material
- reflective plastic
- semi-transparent packaging film

The material should remain mostly invisible as an object.

Instead, it should bend, scatter and reflect the incoming backlight.

It creates:

- blurred foreground light streaks
- transparent reflections
- champagne-colored haze
- pale pink flare
- peach-orange glow
- soft white veiling
- optical distortions
- stretched reflections
- broken highlights
- refracted light
- irregular luminous fragments
- subtle prism effects
- soft foreground blur

These optical elements are very close to the lens and therefore strongly out of focus.

IMPORTANT:

Do not arrange the optical effects neatly around the subject.

Allow them to overlap the face.

Some reflections may pass across:
- an eye
- cheek
- lips
- forehead
- nose

Some parts of the image may become blurred or partially obscured.

This should feel spontaneous and imperfect.

The photographer should feel like they are intentionally shooting THROUGH something.

Everything should appear created physically inside the camera rather than digitally added afterward.

CAMERA:
High-end fashion and beauty editorial photography.

85mm portrait lens look or equivalent medium-format portrait perspective.

Very shallow depth of field.

Focus precisely on one eye, cheek texture or skin surface.

Some eyelashes may be sharply resolved while surrounding areas fall softly out of focus.

The optical foreground must remain heavily blurred.

Use:
- subtle analog softness
- realistic lens diffusion
- fine film grain
- gentle highlight bloom
- slight halation
- natural optical imperfection
- subtle exposure breathing
- delicate falloff

The final image should feel like high-end medium-format film photography scanned beautifully.

Do not make everything clinically sharp.

TEXTURE TENSION — CRITICAL:
The visual beauty comes from the tension between:

hyper-real skin texture
and
partially blown-out optical light.

Pores remain visible underneath a wet luminous surface,
while lens flare occasionally erases or softens parts of the face.

This contrast is essential.

The viewer should simultaneously feel:

“I can almost touch the skin”

and

“I cannot completely see her.”

COLOR PALETTE:
Cream
ivory
champagne
warm beige
soft nude
pale peach
muted blush pink
subtle warm brown

Low saturation.

Soft warm neutral grading.

Avoid excessive orange.

Avoid strong yellow.

Avoid candy pink.

The overall color should feel expensive, quiet, natural and restrained.

MOOD:
Intimate.
Quiet.
Feminine.
Sensual without being sexual.
Private.
Softly melancholic.
Dreamlike but realistic.
Emotionally distant yet tender.

It should feel like a fleeting private moment caught between:

skin,
light,
memory
and silence.

The photograph should feel less like a beauty advertisement and more like a fragment of a memory.

TYPOGRAPHY:
Add only ONE very small emotional English sentence.

Use:

“Some feelings shimmer before they fade.”

Typography should be:
- very small
- elegant
- understated
- thin serif
- slightly italic
- refined editorial typography
- subtle letter spacing
- warm ivory / muted beige / soft brown

Place it discreetly in available negative space.

The text should never dominate the image.

It should feel discovered after looking at the photograph, not immediately noticed.

Do not use large headline typography.

SIGNATURE:
Add a very small signature:

Hu·X

Place it subtly near the lower area of the composition.

Use the same restrained editorial sensibility.

The signature must feel like an artist’s mark, not a logo.

FINAL FEEL:
Luxury beauty editorial photography.
Cinematic realism.
Poetic minimalism.
Tactile hydrated skin.
Wet luminous skin surface.
Real pores.
Warm backlight.
Optical refraction.
In-camera lens flare.
Analog halation.
Soft foreground distortion.
Quiet emotional tension.
Experimental fashion photography.
Sophisticated restraint.

The photograph should look genuinely photographed, not AI generated.

Avoid:
plastic skin,
beauty filter,
airbrushed skin,
perfect digital smoothness,
over-retouched face,
uniform glass skin,
fake digital shine,
CGI reflections,
fake lens flare,
obvious Photoshop glow,
perfect facial clarity everywhere,
symmetrical beauty portrait,
commercial skincare ad composition,
oversaturated orange tones,
heavy makeup,
large typography,
poster-like graphics,
too many design elements.

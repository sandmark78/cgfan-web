## Prompt

Create a premium dual-fragrance surreal campaign board for a fictional international perfume house named VELORIQ, designed as one vertically stacked two-panel main visual with thin white framing and museum-grade black spacing. Preserve the structural logic of the reference as pure visual grammar only: two horizontal panels, each built around a real woman holding a real perfume bottle close to the face, but push the composition away from conventional perfume-ad layout and toward an international surreal key visual. The perfume bottles remain the absolute commercial hero in both panels, while the women function as emotional vessels of scent, light, and atmosphere rather than standard beauty models.

Top panel: VELORIQ Ember. A mature woman with dark hair and natural freckles appears in an intimate side-facing beauty profile, holding a square amber-glass bottle beside her lips and cheek with elegant fingers. Her face must feel close, alive, and luminous, but not like ordinary commercial portraiture. Push the image toward sensorial surrealism: warm amber light seems to bloom from within the glass, reflecting across her skin as if the fragrance is tinting the air around her. The bottle is extremely realistic and product-dominant: thick glass walls, rich amber liquid, crisp gold-amber refractions, polished metallic cap, refined printed logo, premium commercial sharpness. The woman's anatomy must be correct and graceful, with realistic fingers, five digits, natural lips, fine skin texture, visible pores and freckles, no plastic smoothing, no deformation. The background should dissolve into deep shadow and warm fragrance haze, reducing literal environment and making the bottle-and-face relationship feel more iconic and suspended.

Bottom panel: VELORIQ Lumen. A second mature woman with short natural curls appears in a lighter, more open emotional state, holding a bottle from the same family close to her cheek and chin. This version is cooler, cleaner, and more luminous, but equally surreal: silver-white clarity and pale reflections drift around the glass as if daylight itself has been distilled into perfume. The bottle design is the same product family but with a fresher liquid tone and cooler reflection behavior. Her expression is softly joyful, but restrained and elevated, not casual commercial smiling. Her anatomy, hands, shoulder line, curls, and skin must feel real and premium. The monochrome or near-monochrome tonality of the lower panel should be enriched with subtle silver, smoke, and faint champagne reflections so it feels luxurious rather than flat black-and-white.

Across both panels, reduce the amount of visible text radically. The image must feel almost wordless, with the product, face, and atmosphere carrying the narrative. Use only one large brand statement per panel: "VELORIQ Ember" in the top panel and "VELORIQ Lumen" in the bottom panel, each rendered in a refined fusion of bold modern sans and couture script accent, placed as graphic form in the negative space. No dense scent pyramid blocks, no paragraph copy, no informational clutter, no extra promotional lines. If any micro-text appears, it should be nearly invisible and editorial, but ideally avoid it entirely. The board should read like a luxury exhibition diptych for fragrance, not a catalog page.

The emotional structure should be warm-over-cool, dusk-over-morning, skin-over-air, amber-over-light. Top panel expresses dry amber intimacy, warm skin, woody silence, and tactile closeness. Bottom panel expresses luminous breath, fresh clarity, pale musk softness, and open air. The two images must feel like twin states of one perfume universe rather than two separate ads.

Color and material hierarchy: overall composition 55% charcoal-black, deep neutral shadow, and dark breathing negative space; 20% amber bronze, dry gold, and molten honey in the top panel; 15% silver-grey, pearl-white, cool smoke, and pale reflective light in the bottom panel; 10% living skin tones and precise bottle highlights. Material semantics must be explicit and elevated: thick refractive glass, precise label print, metallic cap reflections, amber liquid depth, pale liquid clarity, skin pores, freckled texture, natural curls, moist lips, fingernail sheen, and subtle atmospheric bloom around the bottle edges. The perfume itself must feel like the source of altered reality.

Rendering target: photoreal luxury fragrance diptych, ultra-refined beauty realism, product-dominant hierarchy, reduced typography, surreal atmospheric shaping, world-class print finish, premium border framing, and international gallery-level visual intelligence.

Quality control and exclusion constraints: photoreal only, correct bottle geometry, crisp label placement, correct hand anatomy, five fingers, no extra fingers, no missing fingers, no fused fingers, no broken wrists, no distorted face, no asymmetrical eyes, no waxy skin, no plastic skin, no garbled text, no random letters, no cheap beauty-ad styling, no muddy shadows, no dirty haze, no overdecorated layout, no real brand names, no real person names, no style drift, no AI slop.
